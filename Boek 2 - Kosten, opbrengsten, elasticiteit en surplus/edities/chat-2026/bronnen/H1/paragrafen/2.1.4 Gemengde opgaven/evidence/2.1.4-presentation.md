## Separate teaching example

Slides 3–7 now use **FietsWas**, the teacher-proposed, authored example. Every
slide says **Uitlegvoorbeeld — niet uit het boek**. Its notes identify the
context/data as authored and cite §§2.1.1–2.1.3 only for the underlying methods.
It has no purported textbook exercise number/page and adds no homework.

FietsWas washes at most 40 bicycles per day; every washed bicycle is paid for.
Fixed costs are EUR 36/day, variable costs EUR 2/bicycle, price EUR 5/bicycle.

- TK = 36 + 2Q and TO = 5Q, within 0 ≤ Q ≤ 40.
- At Q = 20: TK = 76, TO = 100, profit = EUR 24/day, GTK = EUR 3.80/bicycle.
  The explanation distinguishes subtraction of two totals from division by Q.
- Break-even: 5Q = 36 + 2Q, then 3Q = 36 and Q = 12; both totals are EUR 60/day.
- Between Q = 20 and 25: MK = (86−76)/(25−20) = EUR 2 and
  MO = (125−100)/(25−20) = EUR 5 per extra bicycle. The denominator is the five
  additional bicycles. Notes require recalculating each interval for a different
  cost table, rather than assuming that its MK also stays constant.
- The native chart marks (12, 60), axis guides, and the vertical EUR 24 profit
  segment at Q = 20 from 76 to 100. Both functions stop at capacity 40.

The earlier worked answers from ShirtSprint, SkateService and FotoFun have

### Exercise, explanation, and worked-example sequence

All questions below are design specifications inside this plan, not student files.
Future printed order: motivating problem → theory → **Uitgewerkt voorbeeld** →
non-heading summary → **Startopgaven** → **Begeleide inoefening** →
**Zelfstandige oefening** → **Doeloefening** → **Denkertje / Bonusopgave** →
**Herhaling / Herhaling en interleaving**. Those seven labels alone use `##` in
student exercise route. Summary has at most five points and forwards to §2.2.2 TO.

Theory design: ask why equal percentage price rises can cause different responses.
Fictional fruit-box seller: P€10→€11, Q100→95 boxes/week; rehearsal-room hire:
P€10→€11, Q100→80 hours/week. At unchanged other conditions, compute −5%/+10%=−0.5
versus −20%/+10%=−2; explain sign separately from size using fig1/fig2 and a small
old/new/% table beside the prose. Available alternatives and ease of postponement
are plausible determinants, not demonstrated causes. Do not infer Ev from visual
slope or import Ei labels. Formula restriction at first use: positive old values,
nonzero price change; observed responsiveness is not a permanent prediction.

W1 worked example, full target chain: Bowlplein raises one session €8→€10;
200→180 sessions/week. Compute %ΔQ=−10%, %ΔP=+25%, Ev=−0.4. Classify and explain
the smaller proportional Q decline. A climbing venue reports Ev=−1.5 for another
rise: elastic and more responsive by magnitude. Plausible explanation: customers
may have more close alternatives for that venue; not proved by these figures.
Show A38 embedded in the canonical A15 steps, ending with a sign/context check.

S1 printed retrieval (3.5 minutes):

- a. “Een fietsbel kost eerst €20 en daarna €22. Bereken de procentuele verandering.”
  Answer: (22−20)/20×100%=+10%; old €20 is base.
- b. “De wekelijkse afzet daalt van 200 naar 180 fietsbelletjes. Bereken de procentuele verandering.”
  Answer: (180−200)/200×100%=−10%; minus sign means decrease.
- c. “Een klant kan naar het werk met een leenfiets of met de bus. Waarom kunnen dit substituten zijn? Als alleen de eigen prijs van de leenfiets stijgt, gaat het dan om de eigen prijs of een andere vraagfactor?”
  Answer: same transport need, alternative choices; own-price change (movement
  along a demand relation, ceteris paribus), not a changed preference. No graph.

S2 current-content check (2 minutes): “Een leerling noemt Ev=−2 prijsinelastisch,
omdat −2 kleiner is dan 1. Verbeter de redenering en vergelijk met Ev=−0,5.”
Answer: magnitude2>1 is elastic; .5<1 is inelastic; both negative describe opposite
price/quantity direction, not the classification. This is brief self-monitoring,
not mastery/diagnosis. Print exact short-route/support note from exercise skill.

G1 (4 minutes): bicycle repair appointments P€20→€22, Q100→95/week. Supply old/new
table, A38 formulas and first percentage completed. Learner completes price +10%,
Ev−0.5, classification and ordinary meaning. For base/sign difficulty print the
four-step A38 reminder, not a web reference.
G2 (6 minutes): two independent venues change their own prices in the same week.
Arcade P€5→€6,Q200→140/week; swimming P€5→€4,Q200→220/week. Provide observations
only plus one cue “Vergelijk procentuele reacties, niet aantallen.” Ask (a) analyse
arcade, (b) analyse swimming, (c) compare both sensitivities, (d) refute “Een even
grote procentuele prijsverandering in absolute waarde betekent altijd dezelfde
prijsgevoeligheid” and offer a plausible explanation for the differing responses.
Answers: arcade −30%/+20%=−1.5 elastic; swimming +10%/−20%=−0.5 inelastic; same
absolute percentage price movement, different response magnitude. Sign retained
in both. These simultaneous but separate markets confront the classification
distinction without adding confounded same-market causal inference or Ei/Ek.
Scaffolding fades from G1's completed first step to G2's single comparison cue.
Print neutral skip: “Heb je deze hulp niet nodig? Ga dan verder met Zelfstandige oefening.”

I1 independent (11 minutes), no hints/formula card: Skatehal raises entry €10→€12,
visits400→280/week; Podiumhuis reports Ev−0.6 for another rise. Ask a signed
percentages/Ev calculation, b classification + meaning, c classify/compare other
venue, d one plausible context explanation and its limit. Answers −30%/+20%=−1.5,
elastic; comparator inelastic; perhaps more alternative recreation for skatehall,
not established by the observations. Same chain as target; no answer-giving diagram.

Target (9 minutes): exact frozen nine-point task above, no hints or formula card
beyond its own authorized context. Bonus (8 minutes outside core): critique “Deze
Ev blijft volgend jaar bij elke prijs gelijk.” Strong response distinguishes this
period/change from future circumstances and names a plausible changing alternative;
criteria: correct scope, plausible mechanism, no unsupported certainty. No new
calculus or elasticity formula. Closing review (5 minutes, homework): H1 price
€20→€25 and back asks why +25% versus −20%; H2 classify own-price change versus
a cheaper substitute in a fresh umbrella/raincoat context. Answers use old bases;
own-price movement versus substitute-price demand shift, all else equal.

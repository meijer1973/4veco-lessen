### Exercise, explanation, and worked-example sequence

#### Instruction design and conditions

Open with a small theatre evening: at €5 per visitor, a full cash box does not
yet tell the organizer whether costs are covered. State one evening, Q paying
visitors (0–30), TK=60+2Q, each ticket €5, all visits paid, constant model over
that range. Use the same case throughout theory to avoid extraneous context
switches. State that function drawings are continuous models although actual
visitors are whole people.

Before new dependent operations, print a compact recall box: substitute Q into
TK, distinguish € per evening from € per visitor, divide by positive Q,
maintain equality when moving terms, plot two coordinates on uniformly spaced
axes. Reference the actual earlier paragraphs, without claiming secure mastery.
Worked reminders are present in print even when optional exercises are skipped.

| Theory step | Teaching move and dual coding | Checked anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define total revenue/omzet, TO=P×Q; verbal sale → short two-column table → TO line | Q0/10/20/30 gives TO0/50/100/150; fig1 |
| 2 | Define average revenue GO=TO/Q, Q>0; show cancellation beside table column €/visitor | Q10 gives50/10=5; Q20 gives100/20=5; GO at0 undefined; do not add a per-unit line to total-money axes |

| 5 | At fixed Q read two money heights, subtract vertically; counterexample to shading a profit area | Q30:150−120=30 €/evening; fig4 vertical bracket; no area |

Check substitution; conventional nearest rounding may leave loss |

Do not introduce a universal break-even formula without conditions or an
optimization claim. The only solved cases have positive constant P greater
than constant variable cost and a crossing inside the given range. Verbal
model conditions accompany first formulas; Q0 is legal for totals, never GO.
No market price-axis convention is applied to the TK/TO graph: its vertical
axis is money per period, not price per item.

Misconception box pairs tempting and correct reasoning: “Er komt €150 binnen,
dus de winst is €150” versus “€150 is TO; trek TK af”; “rond 3⅓ naar 3 af”
versus “controleer het eerste gehele aantal zonder verlies”; “kleur het vlak
tussen de lijnen” versus “lees op één Q het verschil in euro verticaal”. Explain
why cash inflow, familiar nearest rounding and visible enclosed space tempt
the wrong answer. G3/G4/G5 and I6 deliberately surface these errors.

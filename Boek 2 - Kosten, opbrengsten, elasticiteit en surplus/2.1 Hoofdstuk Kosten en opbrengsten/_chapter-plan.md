## Shared contexts and model boundaries

De Korenaar in211: one month, Q=500–1,000, unchanged production capacity;
huur/verzekering440 + connection/subscription60 gives TCK500 €/month;
ingredients/packaging0.70 + oven usage0.10 gives TVK=0.80Q €/month.
At Q500 totals are500/400/900; at Q1,000 they are500/800/1,300.
GCK/GVK/GTK are1.00/0.80/1.80 then0.50/0.80/1.30 €/brood.
No claim that all electricity is variable or all cost components fixed forever.

§212 deliberately reuses TK=500+0.80Q, adds fixed P=1.50 €/brood and
TO=1.50Q €/month; GO=P only for Q>0. Break-even continuous714.2857…;
715 is the first whole bread count without loss. Graph intercepts describe the
stated algebraic functions; drawing them is not evidence that the211 capacity
assumption was empirically verified down to zero. At Q1,000 profit is a vertical
distance of200 €/month, never a shaded area. No marginal/optimal-output rule.

§213 switches to the frozen Linea/Curva sources. MK and MO are interval averages
per extra product, displayed at right endpoints, never derivatives or the raw
row difference. At Linea's10-product intervals MK3/MO8; Curva's5-product
intervals MK5/15/25 and MO30. Constant price explains constant MO.
Do not infer profit-maximizing output from the final table row or MO=MK.

Before214, §213 explicitly connects interval marginals to the vertical profit
distance taught in212: Δwinst=ΔTO−ΔTK, hence Δwinst/ΔQ=MO−MK over the same
interval. A worked two-interval comparison and one faded practice comparison
show why equal MO can accompany unequal profit increases when MK changes.
For example, over two10-product intervals with ΔTO80 each and ΔTK30 then50,
profit grows50 then30 euros:5 then3 euros per extra product. Use this bounded
identity to explain observed interval growth, not to choose optimal output.
It is explanatory scaffolding for the existing213/214 target chain, not a new
terminal goal or derivative. Budget it by replacing repetitive marginal drills
in the actual <=55-minute213 plan, never by appending unbudgeted core work.

§214 SmoothBox: TCK1,200 €/day, variable2 €/lunchbox, price5 €/lunchbox,
normal capacity700/day. Beyond700 use supplied total-cost table, not a linear
extrapolation of normal costs. Positive vertical profit distance grows fastest
over Q400–700; compare normal production with every spoed interval.
The supplied completed curves remain supplied; students mark, not reconstruct.

## Shared notation, visuals and procedures

Use TK/TCK/TVK, GTK/GCK/GVK, TO/GO, MK/MO consistently; Q is contextual quantity,
not interchangeable with euros. Decimal comma and appropriate precision in
student text; state total €/period versus average/marginal €/product in tables,
axes and answers. Q>0 for average formulas. Use productiecapaciteit and explicitly
bounded assumptions; no new formal proportional/degressive/progressive taxonomy.

Colour conventions: TK/TCK #E67E22, TO #1A5276, GTK #8E44AD; direct labels,
line styles/markers and text meaning remain usable without colour. No decorative
graphs. Text, tables and SVG/PNG share exact values and model bounds.

| 212 | Paired TK/TO lines with break-even and a vertical profit bracket; fully modelled example, faded support, independent construction then exact target. No profit-area shading. |
| 213 | Aligned total/interval tables and Δtotal/ΔQ annotation, with changing interval lengths explicit. Tables, not unrequested calculus/curve construction. |
| 214 | Supplied full base graph and source table; student marks BE and interval. Answer asset overlays only requested marks and explains all comparisons. |

Pin exact asset names/geometry in each paragraph plan before generation.
Use paired paragraph-prefixed SVG/PNG; reproducible sources live in platform.

Unified answer procedure: identify quantities/period → select relation → show
substitution → compute and label units → interpret and bound conclusion.
Classification begins with total cost response to Q within capacity/time.
Cost functions sum constant and per-unit components before substituting.
Averages divide each relevant total by the same Q and explain the unit.
Break-even sets TO=TK, solves and checks both sides, then separately handles
whole units. Marginals select an interval, show both differences and divide by
ΔQ, place at the right endpoint and interpret that interval. Reuse these exact
procedures in worked examples, practice and answers; reduce help, not reasoning.

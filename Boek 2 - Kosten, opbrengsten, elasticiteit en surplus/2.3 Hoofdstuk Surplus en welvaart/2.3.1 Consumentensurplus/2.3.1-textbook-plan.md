#### Explanation and misconception architecture

Opening context: four people each consider one pottery-workshop seat, with WTP €18, €14, €10 and €6; given price €10, capacity sufficient, equality purchases allowed. Teach purchase comparison first; three seats trade, buyer gaps €8, €4, €0 sum to €12; payment €30, combined WTP €42. The nonbuyer is excluded, not assigned negative surplus. Names/roles will be varied without stereotyping. This is an explicitly discrete example, **not** the source of any later straight-line geometry.

New formal definition follows: advantage expressed in euros as WTP minus actually paid price per bought unit; aggregate adds these differences for all realized purchases. “Voordeel” is not a payout or accounting profit. A small data table + a price/gap diagram make the sum inspectable. Then explicitly change representation: the next model treats the ordered willingness to pay as a continuous linear demand curve. Its area adds tiny quantity slices; the triangle is a model total, not an exact sum reconstructed from the four-person table. No integral or demand-derivation proof is required.

Theory's continuous demonstration: book-fair admission `P=40−0,5Q`, given P=10, Qd=60; sufficient admissions go to highest-WTP buyers. Relevant range0≤Q≤80, 0≤P≤40. Progressively reveal axes and intercepts, then line/price/quantity, then CS. Explain the upper boundary (WTP), lower boundary (payment per unit), quantity bound (60 sold) and left boundary Q0. Cross-check only in instruction: CS=½×60×30=€900, payment=10×60=€600, model WTP=€1500; WTP−payment=CS. Demand-area is WTP, payment is a rectangle, neither is accounting profit. This cross-check is explanatory, not a sixth required target operation.

Explicit wrong/right comparison, then an exercise response: tempting claim “€10×60 is consumentensurplus” confuses what buyers pay with the gap above payment; show why the correct height is40−10. A second warning uses Qd, not Q*, because no supply relation established equilibrium. The total€900 does not mean each buyer receives€900 or that all buyers benefit equally. At fixed demand and lower given price, within the same full-sales assumptions, the model CS increases; no universal claim beyond those conditions. Bonus below challenges simultaneous changes in price and actual allocation, without new supply/policy theory.

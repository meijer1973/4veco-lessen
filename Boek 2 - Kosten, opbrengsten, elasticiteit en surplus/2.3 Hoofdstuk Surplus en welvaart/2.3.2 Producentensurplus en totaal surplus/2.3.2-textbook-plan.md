### Model conditions, data and procedures

New232 continuous markets use an explicitly competitive simplified market
model: same uniform transaction price, identical service/product units,
highest-WTP demand units matched with lowest-MC supply units, feasible output
through the stated domain, no external effects or trading costs. Curves are
linear modelling choices and ceteris paribus, not facts inferred from a few
people. State these conditions beside first functions, not in a distant note.
Supply is the MC of each successive unit over the entire domain, including
units not traded at equilibrium. This is not a derivation from an individual
firm's profit-maximizing choice or from a coarse213 interval average.

Use Q as market units (or units per stated day), P as €/unit, CS/PS/TS as euro
over that market/period. Both axes name units. Q is not thousands and prices
are not total euro amounts. Supply has a positive P intercept. Only quantities
actually traded and the declared allocation contribute to realized surplus.

Discrete individual demonstration and continuous market area are separate
models. Three exact unit gaps may be summed arithmetically; a continuous curve
represents an integral/area model, not the exact discrete sum of Q ordinal
integers. No calculus is asked. In the frozen table, use the supplied marginal
values at Q50/Q70 exactly as the target defines its extra-unit comparison;
do not silently replace them with Q51/Q71 or claim their point values are exact
finite integrals over [50,51]/[70,71]. Explain this teacher-facing convention
and pupil-facing distinction once: the smooth model assigns a marginal
per-unit comparison at each Q; its total surplus is an area.

Canonical steps (same order in explanations, worked examples and full answers):

1. **Kies bron en voorwaarden.** Name variables/units/domain, actual trades and
   allocation; select functions for equilibrium, graph for regions, table for
   given marginal comparisons. Ignore irrelevant receipts when costs are needed.
2. **Bereken en controleer het evenwicht.** Set inverse demand= inverse supply
   at the same Q; solveQ, substituteP, verify in both. This is the same Book1
   equality idea expressed as P(Q), explicitly taught rather than assumed.
3. **Markeer de gebieden.** Add the equilibrium dot/projections and horizontal
   transaction price; CS above price/below demand; PS below price/above supply;
   both only from0 to tradedQ. Markings never require redrawing supplied curves.
4. **Bereken de bedragen.** Identify perpendicular base and price differences;
   CS=1/2×Qe×(demand intercept−Pe), PS=1/2×Qe×(Pe−supply intercept), TS=CS+PS.
   This triangle rule is specifically for these linear equilibrium regions.
   Show formula, substitution, unit and why; no triangle shortcut for arbitrary
   constrained trade/price cases, which belong to233.
5. **Vergelijk de marginale bedragen en begrens de conclusie.** At the namedQ,
   identify WTP andMC; subtract WTP−MC. Positive adds TS, negative reduces it;
   zero at equality. For descending demand and ascendingMC all gains before
   equilibrium and losses after explain the maximum over the given range.
   Payment cancels between buyer and seller; TS is not equal distribution or
   a complete social-welfare judgment.

PS definition: received price minus MC/minimum supply price for each traded
unit in this model. Aggregate PS is not automatically profit: fixed costs are
not supplied/represented by that marginal area. Never assume absent fixed-cost
data mean zero. Profit maximization, MO=MK output choice, Pareto definitions,
DWL formulas, policy mechanics, externalities and fairness proof are non-goals.

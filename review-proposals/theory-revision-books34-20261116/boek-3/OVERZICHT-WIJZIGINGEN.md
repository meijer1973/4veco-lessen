## What was and was not changed

Changed: the explanation pages and the worked example of the eleven theory paragraphs, 38 pages in total.

Not changed:

- All exercises, from *Startopgaven* onward: byte-identical in every manuscript.
- All *Lesdoelen*: identical. They were clear, and they are repeated in the teacher book and target records.
- All figures, their numbers, captions and the page they stand on.
- Chapter openers, the mixed paragraphs (3.1.6, 3.2.4, 3.3.4), chapter overviews, glossary, formula pages, front matter.
- Page count, page numbers, page titles, outline (21 entries) and all 66 internal links.
- Stylesheet, fonts and components. No new box types, no new figures.

Every page still fits its own page; no text moved to another page.

## The principles behind the rewrite

1. **Every result gets its reason.** Where the text said *what* happens, it now also says *why*.
2. **Concrete before general.** The example comes first, the formula or rule after it.
3. **Define before use.** Terms and symbols are introduced before they appear in a formula or worked example.
4. **Say it once.** Repeated derivations were cut; the space went to missing reasoning.
5. **Link to what students know.** One line back to Book 1 or 2 where a method is reused.

Theory text grew about 11% in words (10,178 to 11,335) inside the same pages, using white space that was already there.

## Changes per paragraph

Page numbers are those of the complete book.

### 3.1.1 Belastingen: wig en nieuw evenwicht (p. 6–9)

- p. 6: explains why two prices are needed before naming them, and puts *belastingwig* into words. The term previously appeared only as a formula label.
- p. 7: says first that V and A themselves do not change, then why supply is rewritten in the buyer price (one price axis). The graph reading is now three numbered steps.
- p. 8: the table now gets read. At Q = 20 the gap is larger than the tax, so trade can still grow; at Q = 60 sellers fall € 4 short; only at Q = 40 does the tax fit exactly. This is the logic of the new equilibrium, which the old text asserted. Added why the quantity falls.
- p. 9: step 3 of the worked example says what to substitute where. Step 4 and *Onthouden* now state the conclusion the lesson is about: remitting is not the same as bearing the burden.

### 3.1.2 Belastingdruk en welvaartsverlies (p. 14–17)

- p. 14: the page opened with a formula in symbols. It now starts from the fruit-cup numbers, names the burden, then gives the formula. *Geen verdwenen geld* says plainly that the € 160 moves and is not lost.
- p. 15: one line back to surplus in Book 2, then what is new (two prices). The welfare loss is described as the triangle that is left over and that nobody receives.
- p. 16: the single-unit argument at Q = 50 is tightened into a clear chain.
- p. 17: opens with the question the page answers. The explanation of why less price-sensitive buyers bear more is now a mechanism (fewer buyers drop out, so sellers can pass the tax on) and is contrasted with market A.
- p. 18 (worked example): unchanged.

### 3.1.3 Subsidies (p. 23–25)

- p. 23: symbols are defined before the figure and formula, not after.
- p. 24: adds why the quantity rises. The paragraph that anticipated the welfare loss duplicated p. 25; it is now a short pointer.
- p. 25: the single-unit argument ends with the point that matters: the subsidy bridges the € 2 shortfall but does not remove it.
- p. 26 (worked example): unchanged. This page and p. 25 had almost no room.

### 3.1.4 Maximumprijs (p. 32–33)

- p. 32: the text now explains why a shortage appears and why it stays (the price may not rise), and names the three quantities the learning goal asks students to distinguish.
- p. 33: explains why a ceiling above the equilibrium price changes nothing. The order is now binding, method, short side, allocation. The *korte kant* rule gets its reason: a sale needs a buyer and a seller. The allocation passage states that the price can no longer decide who gets the product.
- p. 34 (worked example): unchanged.

### 3.1.5 Minimumprijs en quota (p. 39, 41)

- p. 39: introduces *aanbodoverschot* in the running text and explains why it persists. Mirrors the reasoning for the maximum price.
- p. 41: explains why the price rises under a quota (buyers want 60 at the old price, there are 40) and why producers do not expand at € 10.
- p. 40 and 42: unchanged. These were already clear.

### 3.2.1 Volkomen concurrentie: kenmerken (p. 54–56)

- p. 54: new short section *Waarom volgt de onderneming de marktprijs?* with the two halves of the argument: not higher (identical product, known prices) and not lower (it can already sell everything). This reasoning was only in the worked example, yet it is what the target exercise asks for.
- p. 55: GO and MO are now introduced on the theory page, tied to the horizontal line. They previously appeared for the first time in the worked example. The warning box says why market demand still slopes down.
- p. 56: step 3 of the worked example says in words what is calculated.

### 3.2.2 Marginale kosten en de afgeleide (p. 62–64)

- p. 62: the paragraph had no opening problem; it now has one. **The table was replaced.** The old table compared three unrelated numbers. The new one shrinks the step from q = 60 (20 kg: € 9,00; 10 kg: € 8,50; 1 kg: € 8,05) so students see the values close in on € 8. That makes "MK at one point" something they can see, without a limit proof. All numbers follow from the same cost function and were recomputed.
- p. 63: adds what each term of TK means for MK, and links the check at q = 60 back to the table.
- p. 64: the worked example explains why the interval and point values differ.

### 3.2.3 Winstmaximalisatie (p. 69–72)

- p. 69: the text referred to MK before giving TK; the order is fixed. The table discussion now ends with the question the next page answers.
- p. 70: states the rule in words first (expand while an extra unit earns more than it costs), then solves MO = MK, then checks both sides.
- p. 71: connects total profit to profit per kg with the numbers, before the rectangle formula.
- p. 72: *Onthouden* was a string of arrows and semicolons. It is now five short sentences in the order of the method.

### 3.3.1 Waarom landen handelen (p. 88–91)

- p. 88: clearer wording of the two viewpoints; one sentence on what goes wrong when a country makes everything itself.
- p. 89: the step from absolute to comparative advantage was abstract. Added a short everyday comparison (a doctor who also types faster than her assistant) and an explicit link to opportunity cost from Book 1. No numbers and no production table, as the outline requires.
- p. 90: says why specialisation yields more with the same resources, and marks the difference between comparative advantage and competitive position.
- p. 91: two short additions in the worked example. The source text is unchanged, because the answers quote it.

### 3.3.2 Wereldmarktprijs, import, export en welvaart (p. 97–101)

- p. 97: explains why the world price becomes the home price, from the buyer's and the producer's side.
- p. 98–99: the import and export cases now both state why the home price follows.
- p. 100: explains why total surplus rises with imports: what producers lose reaches buyers as a lower price, and buyers gain more on top. Stays qualitative.
- p. 101: step 1 of the worked example gives the reason for importing.

### 3.3.3 Protectionisme (p. 108–111)

- p. 108: explains why home producers can also charge € 25, and that the foreign producer receives no more than before.
- p. 109: the text stated that total surplus falls. It now gives the two causes: extra home production costs more than the world price, and buyers give up units worth more than the world price.
- p. 110: explains why imports disappear when the tariff is too high, and that the home price is then € 30, not € 35.
- p. 111: step 1 of the worked example derives the home price.
- p. 107: unchanged.
